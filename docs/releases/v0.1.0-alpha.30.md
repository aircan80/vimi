# VibeMiner v0.1.0-alpha.30

This update brings many more creatures to life and makes the Far view distance a
setting. Creatures that used to stand frozen in their rest pose now play their
Stand idle: in build 1.60.1.70862, the creature previews play a Stand for 782 of
1,050 sampled creature models, up from 615 in alpha.29. In the World, creatures
with camera-facing parts now stand too, with those parts left out as on World
objects, and many creatures that drew nothing now draw with their base texture,
their glows or their translucent parts. Creatures that already drew can now
show glowing and translucent parts too. Previews draw upright sprites such as flames turning about their upright axis.
The World Explorer's Escape menu gains a remembered Near/Far view distance
choice, and the zone name now shows right at spawn. Library storage did not
change: no schema version or table changed since alpha.29, so an alpha.29
library opens as it is, without migration. Saved model previews are rebuilt
once, the first time each model is opened (#619).

The creature changes were measured on real data with the measurement probe's
display census and covered by unit tests. The new cylindrical card shader passed
the GPU material proof on Vulkan on Linux, and its Direct3D 11 version was built
in CI and verified offline. The owner's on-screen checks on Windows with
Direct3D 11 are still pending for this release and for alpha.29, and nothing new
was run on Metal; see Known limits at the end.

## Creatures: Stand idle

Creatures stay opt-in in the World. The changes below apply to the creature,
mount and display previews and to World creatures. Counts are from the display
census over 1,050 creature models with a static preview in 1.60.1.70862.

- Bone keys on a global clock of length zero are now read. Such a clock never
  advances, so the bone holds its first key, as the preview's playback already
  did. 58 models that were refused as having unordered keys now play their
  Stand (#607).
- A Stand that is an alias of another sequence now plays the sequence its alias
  chain ends at. 15 of 22 such models now play their Stand; 2 more resolve to a
  sequence without bone keys and keep the rest pose, which is their Stand
  (#609). Broken alias chains are refused with their own message.
- Creature displays whose model names bone files now play the model's base rig.
  Bone files are character customization options, and a creature display has
  no customization choice. 39 of 42 such models now play their Stand. Character
  displays and the Races preview are unchanged (#614).
- Creature displays whose bones live in an external skeleton file now play the
  skeleton's own Stand. 21 of 22 such models now play it; the remaining one has
  345 bones, above the 256-bone idle limit (#612, #615). Parent skeletons are
  not read; none of the census models needs one.
- Camera-facing (billboard) bones with translation or scale keys, such as
  pulsing glows and drifting sprites, are now admitted. Their cards keep facing
  the camera while they move and change size. 24 of 56 such models now play
  their Stand; the rest keep the rest pose with a more precise reason (#616).

## Creatures: in the World

- **Camera-facing parts left out.** The World does not draw camera-facing parts
  on creatures, as on World objects. World creatures no longer use the
  preview's stricter rule for those parts: a model with billboard bones the
  preview cannot orient now stands in its Stand pose in the World, with the
  camera-facing triangles left out. In the census, World Stand poses go from
  772 to 835 models. The creature status line says how many placements are
  drawn this way, for example "2 with camera-facing parts left out" (#618).
- **First texture per surface.** World creatures now bind their surfaces by the
  same rule as World objects: the first texture of each section's first simple
  diffuse batch, opaque or alpha-keyed. Before, a creature drew only the
  surfaces the preview binds, so a batch with an environment or add layer, a
  colour track, unlit flags or a texture transform drew nothing. In the census,
  887 of 1,050 models now have a surface the World can draw, against 640
  before. As on World objects, shine layers, colour tints and unlit flags are
  not applied, and a part the client fades in could show. The status line
  notes "first texture per surface" when such creatures are drawn. The
  previews are unchanged (#620).
- **Glows.** A section that no opaque or alpha-keyed batch binds now draws its
  first additive batch in the World objects' additive pass. Spirits, wisps,
  elementals and other glow-only creatures now appear, and 117 models that
  already drew gain glowing parts such as eyes, auras and crystals. In the
  census, drawn models go from 887 to 929, and refusals from 161 to 119. Glows
  whose texture weight fades in or pulses are left out, since a still draw has
  no right value for them. Glows are drawn unlit (#623).
- **Translucent parts.** A section that no opaque, alpha-keyed or additive batch
  binds now draws its first alpha-blended batch in the World objects' blended
  pass, lit unless the material is unlit. In the census, drawn models go from
  929 to 942, and refusals from 119 to 106; about 70 models that draw now show
  blended parts such as veils, wings and translucent shells. Blended parts whose
  texture weight fades are left out (#625).
- In both cases an opaque or alpha-keyed batch still claims its section first,
  so surfaces that drew before are unchanged. Humanoid NPC appearances still
  leave additive and blended surfaces out (#623, #625).
- On a library that has not stored the newly used textures, those creatures are
  refused as having an unavailable texture until the files are stored (#620,
  #623, #625).

## Creature preview: upright sprites

Previews now draw cylindrical billboard cards: sprites that turn only about the
model's upright axis, such as flames, foliage and glows, and the rarer cards
that turn about Y (#619). From eye level they look as before; seen from above,
an upright sprite stays upright and gets shorter instead of tilting toward the
camera. 10 more models now play their Stand in the preview. Saved previews are
rebuilt once because the stored card format changed. Lock-X cards are still
refused.

## World Explorer: view distance and zone

- **View distance setting.** The Escape menu's Display page has a new choice
  under Object textures: "View distance: Near (3 x 3 tiles)" or "Far (5 x 5
  tiles)". It is remembered between runs and defaults to Near. Changing it
  restarts the stream at the new size. The launch flag
  `-Dvimi.world.viewDistance=far` still forces Far for that session, and then
  the choice is disabled (#611).
- **Far uses less memory than before on High textures.** A Far session on High
  object textures used the larger Near splat masks; it now keeps the smaller
  ones (#611).
- **Inspection on Far.** Clicking terrain while on Far reported "Inspection
  failed: Invalid terrain-area query bounds". It now names the zone (#608).
- **Zone at spawn.** The camera starts on a chunk corner, where the area lookup
  reported an overlap, so the zone text read "Terrain area unavailable" and the
  zone's music and ambience did not start until the camera moved. The zone now
  shows right at spawn (#610). The label still reads "Terrain area
  unavailable" for other lookup outcomes.
- The terrain memory bound for each view distance now counts splat masks, the
  splat layer array and the horizon. Nothing drawn changed (#606).

Measured with the probe on Vulkan on Linux in Elwynn Forest and Stormwind, Far
fills all 25 tiles in about 6.5 s against 3.0 s for Near's 9, with unchanged
frame cadence and 123 to 154 MiB more GPU memory (#608). Near stays the default.

## World Explorer: rendering

Nothing drawn changes. The sorted pass for glass and other blended surfaces
reuses the frame's visibility answer instead of testing each placement again,
and the probe reports how many draws it issues (#617).

## Storage

Library storage is unchanged: no schema, user version or table changed since
alpha.29. Saved model previews carry a new revision and are rebuilt once
(#619). World creature surface bindings are saved under their own key, next to
the preview's (#620). That key's rule revision changed with the glow and
blended rules, so World creature bindings are rebuilt once (#623, #625).

## Measurement and tests

- The measurement probe no longer fails when no mode is given, and it now
  honours `-Pvimi.world.viewDistance=far`. The measurement baseline gained a
  Near against Far section (#608).
- The display census gained World Stand and World material sections (#618,
  #620), a breakdown of refused models by their fewest blockers (#623), and a
  count of drawn models by their glow and blended surfaces (#625).
- New tests cover the glow and blended rules, the order in which a section's
  batches claim it, and a World binding that is not saved when a texture weight
  cannot be read (#623, #625).
- A bounded reader for external skeleton files was added, with tests (#612).
- The GPU UI proofs no longer count a renderer child that has exited but not
  yet been collected as a live renderer, which failed one proof on every run
  on Linux (#613).
- A storage retry test that could fail on slow Windows CI runners uses a longer
  bound (#621).

## Known limits

- **Owner checks on Windows.** The creature changes (#607, #609, #614, #615,
  #616, #618, #620, #623, #625) were measured on real data with the display census and
  covered by unit tests, without a GPU proof of their own. The cylindrical card
  shader (#619) passed the GPU material proof on Vulkan on Linux. The view
  distance setting (#611) and the zone at spawn (#610) were tested on Linux.
  The Direct3D 11 shader was built in CI and verified offline, but these
  on-screen checks on Windows with Direct3D 11 are still pending:
  - creatures that used to stand frozen now idling, with nothing jumping,
    flipping, exploding or tilting, including alias Stands, bone-file rigs and
    skeleton rigs (face and hands) (#607, #609, #614, #615);
  - glow cards pulsing or drifting while staying camera-facing in the previews
    (#616);
  - World creatures with flames or glows standing with those parts left out
    (#618);
  - World creatures that were missing now drawn with their base texture, and
    how far the missing shine, tints and fades show (#620);
  - glow-only creatures appearing as soft glows that add light, with no dark
    boxes or fully opaque quads; glows on creatures that already drew, with
    nothing showing that the client hides; and unlit glows that look too
    bright at night (#623);
  - translucent creatures and parts with no dark halos, fully opaque quads or
    parts showing through solid geometry; lit blended parts by day and at
    night; and blended parts of one creature flickering or popping in front of
    each other as the camera turns (#625);
  - upright sprites in the preview from eye level and from above (#619);
  - switching between Near and Far on the Display page, the remembered choice,
    and the disabled choice with the launch flag (#611);
  - Far filling smoothly and clicking an outer-ring tile naming the zone
    (#608);
  - the zone text and its music right at spawn (#610).

  Carried over from alpha.29 and still pending:
  - the stretched non-square textures and the formerly untextured tiles (#576,
    #572);
  - water and lava: recorded ocean and river colours, coasts, and opaque lava,
    fel and tar (#586, #594);
  - the recorded light on terrain, objects and the horizon, by day and at night
    (#591, #592, #597);
  - glass and water panes in one object drawing in the right order (#574);
  - creatures' body parts under the new geoset default (#599);
  - the recorded sky in native window presentation (#567), whose proof has not
    been run on Windows yet;
  - splat blending with `-Dvimi.world.splat=true`: the fade band staying put
    while turning, no seam from a neighbouring chunk, and no tiles stuck
    untextured on the high tier at Near (#581, #583);
  - ambience starting without an extra pause when flying between zones (#578).

  The Metal shaders are built, and nothing new was run on macOS hardware.
- **Creatures still in the rest pose.** In the previews, billboard bones with
  child bones or rotation keys, members not rigidly bound, lock-X cards and a
  few parent-inheritance flags still refuse the Stand. In the World, 3 models
  with ignore-parent flags and the two models above the bone limit stay in the
  rest pose. Models whose Stand records no bone keys show their rest pose
  (#384).
- **Creatures not drawn in the World.** 106 of 1,050 census models still have no
  surface the World can draw. By their fewest blockers: 54 have glow or
  blended batches whose texture weight fades or pulses, 25 use character
  texture slots on displays without extended appearance, 11 have empty monster
  skin slots, 6 use edge-fade or environment combiners, 9 have no batch blocker
  but lose their sections to geosets or hidden sections, and 1 has an
  unavailable texture (#361, #384).
- **Glow and blended parts in the World.** Colour tracks are not applied as tints
  or alpha, so a blended part can draw more opaque than in the client. Glows
  are drawn unlit; about 10% of the sampled glow batches are not marked unlit,
  so those draw brighter at night than in the client. Blended parts sort per
  part within a creature, so overlapping parts can sort wrongly from some
  angles. A creature shown in its rest pose draws its camera-facing glow and
  blended quads fixed (#361, #623, #625).
- **Bone files** are not applied for character displays, and `.anim` files and
  parent skeletons are not read (#384).
- **Sun direction.** The light's direction is still fixed. The light tables
  record colours, not a direction, and the client's sun path is not pinned yet
  (#177).
- **Water depth.** The shallow-to-deep mix reads the depth byte linearly; the
  client's exact curve and water textures are not used yet (#594).
- **View distance.** Near stays the default; whether Far becomes the default is
  not decided (#173).
- **Opt-in features.** These are Java system properties for testing, not
  settings in the app yet:
  - splat blending: `-Dvimi.world.splat=true`;
  - the recorded sky, fog, light and water light: `-Dvimi.world.sky=recorded`,
    with `-Dvimi.world.sky.time=HH:MM` to pin a time;
  - creatures: `-Dvimi.world.creatures=true`;
  - WMO vertex colours: `-Dvimi.world.wmoVertexColours=true`;
  - high object texture detail: `-Dvimi.world.objectTextures=high`;
  - native presentation into a child window, on Windows only:
    `-Dvimi.world.nativePresent=true`.

  The Far view distance is now a setting; `-Dvimi.world.viewDistance=far` still
  forces it. Terrain lighting is on and can be turned off with
  `-Dvimi.world.terrainLighting=false`.
- **Outland and Northrend** (maps 530 and 571) are not in build 1.60.1.70862,
  so the texture fixes could not be measured there.
- **NPCs.** On 1.60.1.70862, few spawned NPCs have a Creature record, so few
  reach a display, as in alpha.29.
- **macOS:** packages are unsigned and not notarized, and native presentation is
  not available. The signature check on `--image-only` builds was tested with
  `codesign` stubbed and has not been run on a Mac yet (#568).
- **Linux:** packages need the standard GTK and X11 desktop libraries, and native
  presentation is not available. The deb dependencies added in alpha.29 were
  not tested with a clean install.
