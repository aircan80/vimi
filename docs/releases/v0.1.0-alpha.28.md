# VibeMiner v0.1.0-alpha.28

This is the largest World Explorer update so far. The view now fills the window
with a game-style heads-up display, renders at the window's own resolution at
60 frames per second, and can stream a wider neighbourhood. Terrain is lit,
distant land is drawn as a low-resolution horizon, and dungeon and raid
interiors open in place. Far more doodads are drawn, and repeated models are
drawn together, so dense areas cost a fraction of the draws they did. Imported
client hotfix caches now change what the table views show. Library reads are
faster. Library storage keeps its schema version, and no existing table changed;
the only addition is a small World controls table created on first use (#400), so
an alpha.27 library opens as it is, without migration.

The new World drawing was checked on real data with Vulkan on Linux. On Direct3D
11, lit terrain and splat passed the GPU proofs on test data, while instancing and
the horizon still need a run there. Nothing new was run on Metal; see Known limits
at the end.

## World Explorer: the view and controls

The viewport now takes the page (#514). The map list opens as a panel over the
view (Map button or M), and H or Alt+Z hides the whole HUD. Over the view you
get:

- the zone name, with a banner when you enter a new area, and a coordinates pill
  you can copy (#516);
- a row of status icons for rendering, textures, audio, time of day and mouse
  look (#518);
- an inspect card for the object you clicked, which I pins (#519);
- toasts for events such as a bookmark saved or an instance entered (#517);
- a minimap cluster with zoom, a north-up or heading-up toggle, a tracking popup
  for the spawn and role markers, and a ring showing tile streaming (#515).

Escape opens a game menu with Controls, Audio, Display, Bookmarks and Instances
pages (#520), and B opens it on Bookmarks (#524). F3 shows a developer overlay
with stream, render, input and audio pages (#513). F12 saves a screenshot of the
view, without the HUD, to the `screenshots` folder in the app's data directory
(#521). F11 or Alt+Enter shows the view full screen (#415). V, L, N, T and 1 to 5
toggle spawn markers, tracking, the minimap and the time of day, and pick a flight
speed (#523). The in-app key help lists every key.

Movement keys can be rebound, and AZERTY layouts get sensible defaults (#409).
There are five flight speed presets. Mouse look now uses the right button and
captures the cursor, with sensitivity and invert-Y settings, and keyboard turning
is faster (#400).

The image is sharper and smoother:

- Frames render at the viewport's real size instead of 800 × 500 scaled up
  (#425), and are re-requested when the window moves to a screen with another
  scale (#495).
- They reach the screen at up to 60 fps instead of 30 (#387), through a
  PixelBuffer instead of a per-frame copy (#497), with less copying and garbage
  on the way (#496, #399).
- The World target uses 4× multisampling, and textures are filtered
  anisotropically (#389).
- Fog now reaches full strength at the far cut and fades into a sky gradient of
  the same colour, so distant ground no longer ends in a hard edge. Foliage and
  grates use alpha to coverage instead of a hard alpha test (#407).
- One 90° field of view is shared by drawing and picking (#394), and overlays
  and clicks use the camera of the frame actually shown (#412). While terrain in
  the viewing direction loads, the live sky is shown instead of a stale image
  (#417).

View distance is a choice per session (#276). Near, a 3 × 3 tile neighbourhood,
stays the default. Far streams 5 × 5 tiles within fixed GPU budgets, and is
opt-in (see Known limits). Tiles load ring by ring, starting in the direction you
face. A moving camera's tiles ahead of it are loaded first (#493), and a tile you
cross back into is kept rather than cancelled and restarted (#494).

## World Explorer: terrain, water and sky

Terrain is one indexed mesh per tile, culled chunk by chunk (#430), instead of
one instance per triangle with every corner repeated. Terrain is now lit by the
tile's own MCNR normals and MCCV vertex colours, under the same light as
buildings (#404, #487). It is on by default.

Many more tiles get textures. Texture data the client renders is no longer
refused (#397). Whole maps such as Outland and Northrend were drawn untextured,
because their map flags were not the one value that was accepted. Diffuse
textures up to 8 MiB now decode (#435, #428). Before, 10,884 of 50,176 sampled
chunks on Eastern Kingdoms lost their diffuse. Chunks that declare no layers are
named (#428) and count as complete (#446), and a refused diffuse now says why
(#448). The terrain atlas is
filtered over a full mip chain (#257).

Near the camera, terrain can blend its own texture layers instead of the baked
atlas (#344, #435, #438, #477). This splat blending is opt-in for now.

Past the streamed tiles, the map's WDL heights are drawn as a horizon out to six
tiles, about 3,200 yards (#460, #462, #465, #479, #561). Fog reaches out to the
horizon, so terrain, objects and the horizon fade into the sky together.

The sky and fog can come from the recorded light tables for the camera's place
and the local time of day (#350, #486, #541). With this on and the horizon drawn,
a recorded fog that ends before the horizon is kept, so foggy zones stay foggy
(#559). The recorded sky is opt-in.

Water draws sloped and overlapping layers (rivers, waterfalls), whole coast and
open-sea tiles, and layers without vertex heights (#463).

## World Explorer: buildings, doodads and creatures

Objects behind the camera, off to the side, past the far cut or smaller than a
pixel are no longer drawn (#369). The visible placements of each model in a tile
are now drawn together as instances (#557). On the Elwynn and Stormwind routes
with build 1.60.1.70862, measured before the doodad cap below was raised, draws
per frame fell from a median of 910 to 259 in Elwynn. In Stormwind the 95th
percentile fell from 2,311 to 533. Render time on the CPU fell about threefold.
The test frames are pixel-identical with and without instancing.

Each tile can now place up to 4,096 doodads instead of 512 (#440). Around
Goldshire, doodads drawn went from 3,586 (4,014 capped) to 7,565 with none
capped. Doodads from buildings' doodad sets went from 317 to 2,202. The rest of
those are still capped by the limit of 96 distinct doodad models per tile. Before
instancing, the raise roughly doubled Elwynn's draws per frame; instancing
groups those repeated doodads again. Doodads that carry a small camera-facing
card, such as lamps, torches, braziers and many bushes, are drawn, leaving out
only the card (#390).

Object textures:

- They have mip chains and trilinear filtering, so distant objects no longer
  shimmer (#286, #352).
- They reach the GPU in the file's own BC1, BC2 and BC3 blocks, charged at their
  compressed size, which lets the per-tile caps rise (#420, #441, #466).
- When a tile's image budget runs short, images are shared by size and
  downscaled instead of dropped (#284).
- Each level is decoded at most once (#391, #442).

Buildings:

- Newer WMO groups are read, so outdoor buildings on recent maps such as Zephras
  Isle draw (#266).
- Tilted buildings and buildings with MODF flag 0x80 draw with their doodad sets
  (#296).
- Large interior groups and uninterpreted MODD flags are admitted (#288).
- Unlit surfaces such as lit windows, signs and lamps are drawn at full
  brightness, and additive glows and light beams are drawn (#447). Within one
  object, glows are drawn after its glass (#549).
- WMO vertex colours (baked building shading) and the two-sided material flag
  are carried (#472). Vertex colours are opt-in until compared with the client.

Dungeons and raids:

- You can enter a dungeon or raid in place from the World view, with its
  recorded music and ambience (#277).
- Every WMO-only instance interior opens, not just Onyxia's Lair (#281).
- ADT-based instances such as Shadowfang Keep, Deadmines, Zul'Gurub and
  Blackwing Lair draw their interior groups (#337).
- Interior rooms that can't be seen through any portal are no longer drawn
  (#481, #483, #548, #558).
- The dungeon viewer draws WMO materials the way the World view does (#482).

Creatures (opt-in):

- Alpha-keyed hair, fur, wings and leaves draw, the default geoset rule applies,
  and textures can be up to 256 × 256 (#392).
- Creatures stand in their Stand pose instead of the bind pose (#444).
- Each display's chosen geosets are drawn (#452, #461).
- Spawn queries, table refusals and busy previews are counted correctly (#396).
- Humanoid NPCs can be drawn from their race appearance, with their choices,
  clothing and pre-baked texture (#469, #475, #550). See Known limits for what
  this means on current builds.

Quest-giver, vendor, trainer and flight-master markers appear on the minimap
(#349). The Maps browser filters them by faction and shows details on hover
(#339).

## World Explorer: audio

World music and ambience now play on Windows. The device check matched no
Windows mixer (#243), and MP3 tags stopped the decoder (#244, #256, #393).
Mono-only output devices work, with stereo mixed down (#256, #291).

Sound kits use their recorded file weights and volume (#388). Mixed-format
ambience loops without restarting, and day and night follow the clock. Zone
changes cross-fade music and ambience with equal power instead of leaving a gap
of silence (#411). Turning music or ambience on plays the current area at once
(#413), and the next ambience file is opened ahead of time, near the end of the
current one (#476).

## Hotfixes, tables and talents

An imported client hotfix cache now changes what views show (#294). Before,
importing a matching cache changed no table that any view reads. Since then:

- Columns the base tables keep as raw integers decode when their values fit
  (#324). On one Forever 70790 cache, such columns masked about 400 rows in 10
  tables. About 350 of them, in six tables, now decode, including the 12 talent
  prerequisite edges; five partly encrypted or non-fitting tables stay refused.
- An inconclusive check is retried instead of cached, a refused rebuild keeps the
  working overlay, and re-checks are capped (#331, #347).
- The decoder accepts tables of up to 512 columns, such as PlayerCondition's 224
  (#328), and more records (#341). PlayerCondition's own rows stay hidden while
  its base is partly encrypted. Rows carrying an optional key block are shown,
  and other trailing bytes are named (#336).
- Keys delivered through hotfixes join the build's key set (#345).
- A table refused only because of its own pinned views is retried, and an
  unreadable base backs off (#325).
- When the game client updates, its watched cache is offered to the new build
  instead of making you import it again (#303).

Talent descriptions resolve far more of their text:

- tier gates and rank values from TraitTree, and `$m`, `$h` and `$o` (#319);
- arithmetic `${...}` expressions (#332);
- `$n`, `$u`, `$t` and `$a` (#333);
- exact `$/N;` division (#348);
- dummy and proc amounts printed as percentages (#334).

Talent descriptions are also kept when metadata is retried, icons explain
themselves, and Reload works (#289). The SpellDuration supplement is pinned at a
revision that lists 1.60.1.70790 (#322). "Preparing research lookups" now shows
progress and logs how long it took (#351).

## Storage and performance

Library reads:

- They share pooled connections instead of opening one per read (#416).
- They wait for a free worker instead of failing as busy, and a full queue is
  never mistaken for a missing file (#426).
- Opening a file no longer fetches its encoded data twice (#418).

On Windows, an index rebuild works while another process reads the same library
(#480, #501). The object store was validated on native Windows (#258, #263,
#264). Builds that are already prepared can be read without opening the library
as a writer (#471, #488).

The app and the GPU helper run on the ZGC collector, with a bounded helper heap
(#406). Streaming does less repeated work:

- The terrain root parse and the map inventory are reused for tile materials
  (#421).
- A large building's groups are read up to four at a time (#468).
- Tile packets write and read in bulk, without forcing them to disk (#432).
- Reads skip name lookups and format checks they don't need (#419, #427, #467).
- The stream controller acts on events instead of every screen tick (#492), and
  waits on releases instead of polling (#454, #490).

## Measurement and diagnostics

Stage timings across the World pipeline are available with
`-Dvimi.world.stageTimings=true` (#423). A read-only measurement probe runs only
on a marked copy of a library and never on the library the app uses. It reports
tile preparation, hand-off timings, the CPU cull, model reuse and creature
outcomes (#542, #543, #544, #545, #556).

A World view that stays responsive but stops showing new frames is now detected
and explained (#295). A tile or camera the helper rejects no longer ends the
session (#424). Unrelated helper output is ignored instead of failing (#292).
Per-tile object failures (#290) and an unavailable object pass (#245) say why.

## Known limits

- **Backends.** Instancing and the horizon were checked on Vulkan only. On
  Direct3D 11, lit terrain and splat passed the GPU proofs on test data,
  instancing only had its shader inputs checked, and the horizon was not run
  (#557, #479). The Metal shaders are built, and nothing new was run on macOS
  hardware. The Windows audio fixes (#243, #244) were confirmed on Windows with
  real game music.
- **Opt-in features.** These are Java system properties for testing, not settings
  in the app yet:
  - splat blending: `-Dvimi.world.splat=true`;
  - the recorded sky and fog: `-Dvimi.world.sky=recorded`, with
    `-Dvimi.world.sky.time=HH:MM` to pin a time;
  - the Far view distance: `-Dvimi.world.viewDistance=far`;
  - creatures: `-Dvimi.world.creatures=true`;
  - WMO vertex colours: `-Dvimi.world.wmoVertexColours=true`;
  - high object texture detail: `-Dvimi.world.objectTextures=high`;
  - native presentation into a child window, on Windows only:
    `-Dvimi.world.nativePresent=true` (#439, #451).

  Terrain lighting is on and can be turned off with
  `-Dvimi.world.terrainLighting=false`.
- **NPCs.** On build 1.60.1.70862, no humanoid NPC is drawn in the Stormwind and
  Goldshire tiles. The client's Creature table there has 189 rows, and none of
  the 454 NPC IDs spawned in those tiles has a record, so no display is found
  (#556).
- **Doodads inside buildings** are still capped by the 96 distinct doodad models
  per tile. Within one object, see-through surfaces are drawn in file order.
- **macOS:** packages are unsigned and not notarized, and native presentation is
  not available.
- **Linux:** packages need the standard GTK and X11 desktop libraries, and native
  presentation is not available.
