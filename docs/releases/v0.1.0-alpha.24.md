# VibeMiner v0.1.0-alpha.24

World Explorer can play recorded area music and ambience. The World footer has
Music and Ambience toggles, both off by default, with separate volumes and a
Day/Night choice. Vimi follows the table links from the current area or subzone
to its recorded music and ambience kits; the most specific area wins. Tracks
fade in and out over 1.5 seconds, a kit keeps playing across subzones that
share it, music waits the recorded silence between tracks, and ambience loops.
Ogg Vorbis and MP3 files are decoded in a separate, memory-bounded process and
nothing is saved to disk. MP3 decoding adds one new dependency, JavaMP3 1.0.1
(MIT, pure Java); its licence ships with the app. Game time, building
interiors, underwater music, player conditions, positional emitters and the
client's sound-kit mixing are not evaluated. Playback has not been checked by
ear against real game data.

World audio fails more quietly and more safely. With no audio device, Vimi now
says so before it reads a file or starts a decoder. When the output stops
accepting audio or a stream breaks, the channel shows a status and waits for
the next change instead of re-reading and re-decoding in a silent loop. Status
text no longer repeats system error messages that could name local paths.

Maps can show quest giver, vendor, trainer and flight master markers. Turn each
layer on with its own toggle in the Maps footer; all four are off by default.
Roles come only from the build's mapped, already prepared QuestieDB snapshot:
quest givers from recorded quest starts, services from per-flavor NPC flags.
Questie's area percentages are placed through the build's UiMap assignments,
and a position is drawn only when every candidate for that map agrees. The
status line names the Questie release and reports per-layer, unplaced,
conflicting and capped counts. Faction, phase and quest availability are not
evaluated, markers have no hover details, and this has not yet been checked
against a real prepared Questie snapshot.

Preparing file catalog and Preparing common tables do less repeated work. File
lookups search the already verified encoding pages in place instead of
copying and re-hashing a page each time, and catalog and table rows reach the
store in batches rather than one at a time. Table preparation keeps the
verified definitions bundle in memory (about 22.5 MB for the life of the
process) instead of re-inflating it for each table, and skips file name and
format probes it does not need. In a synthetic benchmark, looking up all 1,346
bundled definitions dropped from about 19.6 s to 1.2 s; the other gains are
expected but were not measured end to end. Results are unchanged.

World Explorer can inspect what you click. A still left click, not a drag,
reports what lies under the pointer in the footer. A drawn NPC spawn marker
shows its name, subname, level, rank, position and source record from the same
snapshot, or "name unknown" when the snapshot has none. Elsewhere the click
reports the terrain map, tile, position, distance and recorded area, up to
2,200 yards away. Holes, unloaded tiles and terrain outside the retained height
tiles pick nothing. Buildings, doodads, water and live creatures cannot be
inspected yet, and there is no selection highlight.

The Races model preview now labels a model SD or HD only when the source says
so. The model selector reads, for example, "Male · HD" when the build's
ChrRaces row records a matching standard and high-resolution display pair, and
the line below names the exact source field. Otherwise it reads "Model
definition: not stated" with the reason. Current Forever layouts record no such
pair, so they always show "not stated"; no real build reaches a stated
definition yet. The shown model and its readiness are unchanged.

The dungeon interior view can navigate between a model's recorded groups. A
side panel lists each WMO group in file order with its recorded name ("name not
recorded" otherwise) and flags. Click a group or press Enter to move the camera
to the centre of its recorded bounds; Overview restores the initial framing.
Floors are reported as not linked and the entrance as unknown, because neither
is read from a source yet. If the group list cannot be read, the panel says so.

Groundwork for outdoor buildings in World Explorer: Vimi can now read a tile's
qualifying building exteriors, meshes and opaque textures within fixed budgets,
counting everything it leaves out. Nothing is drawn yet; rendering follows in
a later release. A design note for this work was also added.
